# Law map: trademarks

Navigation, not law. Every act below is where to **look**; before it carries a conclusion it is read in the
official text in the version in force on the task date (`law-check.md`, `version-check.md`). Article numbers and
dates marked *reported* come from an earlier reading and are the first thing to confirm. Fees and office
deadlines change by secondary acts: an amount or a term is never given from memory, only from the office's
current fee list or the act read on the task date.

## Known traps

- **Application is not registration.** No `®` and no "registered trade mark" for an application; before
  registration a letter claims rights "subject to registration" (EU art. 11(2) reasonable compensation; RO art.
  43, *reported*: damages under general law for acts after the publication of the application).
- **Territory.** A Romanian or EU mark does not protect in Moldova, a Moldovan mark not in the EU. An EU mark
  covers Romania. Moldova is reached nationally or through the Madrid Protocol.
- **Registration is protection, not permission.** Using a name needs no registration; the risk of using it is
  an earlier right of someone else. A registration does not cure an earlier conflict.
- **"Available" is never a verdict.** No search proves the absence of earlier rights (unregistered marks, firm
  names, pending filings, other countries). The verdict is the scale of `norms.md` §5 with the sources and the
  date: "no conflicting right found in the sources checked on <date>".
- **OSIM opposition counts from the publication of the admission**, not of the application (*reported*); EUIPO
  from the publication of the application.
- **Representation.** Before EUIPO and OSIM a person or firm without domicile, seat or real establishment in the
  EEA must be represented (EU art. 119(2); RO art. 15(2), *reported*). A Moldovan firm filing directly in the EU
  or Romania needs a representative; the Madrid route through AGEPI may need one only on an objection — check.
- **Secondary copies are not quotes.** WIPO Lex, legislation.gov.uk (UK law, not the EU regulation) and
  summaries help to find a text; a conclusion rests on the official text.
- **A specification too broad** invites a bad-faith attack and non-use cancellation after five years: claim what
  is used or really planned.
